# Stock: TuftStock vs. the suite, side by side

Read-only audit, 24 Nov 2026. Nothing in either codebase was changed by the audit. Rows closed since then (four batches, the last on 24 Nov 2026) are marked **Matched** with the new file references, and the counts below are recounted from the rows.

**How to read the file references**

- **Old** paths are inside `/home/user/tuftstock-inventory` (for example `src/components/ReordersTable.tsx:36`). One supplier has its own email readers there; those files are written as `src/lib/shipments/*-email.ts` and the like, so that no supplier is named in this public file.
- **New** paths are inside `/home/user/maker-business-suite`. To keep the tables narrow:
  - `stock/` means `src/app/w/[slug]/(app)/stock/`
  - `lib/` means `src/lib/stock/`
  - `mig/` means `supabase/migrations/`
- **Matched** means the code for it was found in the suite. **Partly** means some of it was found. **Missing** means it was looked for and not found.
- Size to close: **S** = a day or two, **M** = about a week, **L** = several weeks or a new integration.
- Each row was checked in the code on both sides, not only in TuftStock's documents. Where the two disagree, the tables follow the code (section 6 lists the places).

## 1. Summary

TuftStock has **130** capabilities that a person ordering stock or running the shop would notice. In the suite, **109 are matched**, **3 are partly matched** and **18 are missing**. Sixteen of the missing ones are the Michaels Marketplace connector, which the suite does not have at all; leaving that aside, the score for stock itself is **109 matched, 3 partly and 2 missing of 114**.

The heart of the tool came across whole. The reorder sums are the same code with the same tests (how fast something sells, when it runs out, when to order, how many, smallest orders, seasons of the year, the "how it's doing" labels). So are suggested orders by supplier, purchase orders with shipping, tax and adjustments, checking a delivery in and adding it to the shop's count, tracking numbers typed in by hand, the Spend page, and the supplier balances ledger with its "Paid" ticks and statement checks.

What the suite does not have yet is what TuftStock reads out of email (tracking, invoices, statements and bank notices: that needs permission to read the business's mail, which the suite does not ask for), FedEx tracking (left out on purpose), a bills calendar that keeps itself up to date (the suite hands over a calendar file instead), counts on each read of the shop, and the Michaels connector. A fourth batch (24 Nov 2026) closed the rest: selling seasons with a lift and their order-by cards, the two item switches and the shop-tag rule, the orders list's views, search, shipping column and spreadsheet, renaming, archiving and deleting orders, each order's timeline, the first screen's tiles, parcels and shop read, the late-money tiles and notices on Balances, payments read from QuickBooks, Spend's search, and what the help desk and the home page read from Stock. Emailing an order from the owner's own Gmail, live UPS tracking and the spreadsheet imports came in the third batch. The thing that used to change the numbers quietly at a switch, sold-out days and average costs starting thin, is closed by the imports: past daily counts and past orders can be brought in (`docs/stock-import.md`).

## 4. Recommended build order

Biggest day-to-day effect first. Nothing here needs code written for one customer.

1. _(Built, 24 Nov 2026.)_ **Bring-across tools: suppliers, item planning, and purchase orders from a spreadsheet.** Also the supplier ledger, past daily counts and past deliveries. No package was needed: the CSV reader is the suite's own (`lib/import/csv.ts`). The order import never touches a count (`tests/db/stock-imports.test.ts`).
2. _(Built, 24 Nov 2026.)_ **The small things that make a number wrong or a button unsafe.** Leave "not used any more" suppliers out of the "To order" cards; stop a double press from checking a delivery in twice; offer "try the shop again" when adding to the shop's count failed. All S.
3. _(Built, 24 Nov 2026.)_ **Sold-out days left out of the sales rate.** A nightly count per item, and the sums already know what to do with it. Without it, items that sell out are under-ordered, which is the mistake this tool exists to prevent. Nothing new to buy; no new shop permission.
4. _(Built, 24 Nov 2026.)_ **The order as a PDF, with the business's name, address and logo.** Suppliers get a proper document. The logo and colours can come from the brand kit. _A PDF-drawing package would be a new dependency (TuftStock uses one); ask first._ The print page is the stop-gap.
5. _(Built, 24 Nov 2026; not yet tried against a live Google project.)_ **Email the order from the business's own mailbox, then "resend the changed order".** A Gmail connection that can only send, kept in the vault. Google reviews apps that ask for that permission before other businesses can use it; what to set up is in `docs/gmail-orders.md`. Wording is a config. Sent mail is always something a person pressed.
6. _(Built, 24 Nov 2026.)_ **Average cost from past deliveries, and your own cost for an item.** Order values, Spend and suggested-order totals then match what is really paid, not the shop's cost field.
7. _(Built, 24 Nov 2026.)_ **Changing planning figures in the table, several items at once; adding any item to an order.** Speed for the weekly reorder. The database already has a bulk planning function that no screen uses (`plan_stock_items`).
8. _(Built for UPS, 24 Nov 2026; not yet tried against live UPS.)_ **Live tracking for incoming parcels, and which items are in each.** The workspace's own UPS connection is used (its developer app needs the Tracking product added). FedEx is left out by the owner's decision.
9. _(Built, 24 Nov 2026.)_ **Seasons you set, with the build-ahead cards.** Selling seasons with a lift and an order-by date per supplier and lead-time group, beside the cards for a supplier's closure. Seasons are a config.
10. _(Built, 24 Nov 2026.)_ **The late-money tiles and the hidden payment warning, order search, the timeline, the feed for the help desk.** Also the orders list's views and spreadsheet, renaming and archiving, the two item switches, the shop-tag rule and adopting a supplier's real lead time.
11. **Balances that fill themselves in.** (a) _(Built, 24 Nov 2026; not yet tried against live QuickBooks.)_ Payments from the QuickBooks connection the suite already has: its permission covers bill payments and purchases, and nothing is read until the workspace switches it on. (b) _(Partly built.)_ Bills due as a calendar file to download; a calendar that keeps itself up to date needs either a private subscription link (a decision: it is an address that shows what is owed to anyone who has it) or a new connection that writes into someone's calendar. (c) Still to do: invoices and statements read from email, which needs permission to read the business's mail and must be a general reader whose findings a person confirms, never one reader per supplier.
12. **Tracking read from supplier emails.** Only after 5 and 11c, and only as a general "read tracking numbers out of replies to an order" that any workspace could use. The old reader knows one supplier's phrasing; that knowledge would have to become settings, not code.
13. **The Michaels Marketplace connector, as an optional connection.** See below.

**Michaels, as a connection any workspace could turn on.** It must arrive the way every other integration does: a provider in `src/lib/connections/providers/` behind the `ProviderAdapter` interface, its seller key in the vault, its switches (watch-only mode, cut-over time, per-job switches, held-back units, who is told) as a config, its work done by the worker, and its screens shown only to workspaces that connected it. Nothing in it may name one shop: the brand to list under, how orders are marked, and the price rule are settings with plain defaults. Things to settle first:

- _Shop permissions it needs that the suite does not ask for today:_ creating orders, cancelling orders, and refunding with restock. These are far wider than "add a delivery to the count" and "mark as sent", and every workspace that connects Michaels would have to grant them. Workspaces that don't connect it must never be asked.
- _It is the one place stock is put back in the shop automatically_ (a Michaels cancellation or a returned item). The suite's rule today is that Stock only ever adds a checked-in delivery; this would be a second, named exception, behind its own switch, as it is in TuftStock.
- _Timing._ TuftStock runs it every ten minutes. Whether the suite's worker and background-job allowance cover that for every connected workspace was not checked.
- _Alerts._ Post to Messages with `postAlert()` first; email only if asked, and then through whatever the suite already sends mail with (not checked which service that is).
- _Order of building:_ connect and match listings in watch-only mode; stock and price; orders in and tracking out; cancellations; returns; publishing and listing management last.
- The Michaels seller API itself was not tried from the suite; TuftStock's notes on its quirks (`docs/08-michaels-connector.md`) are the starting point.

Until it exists, the realistic plan is to leave TuftStock running for Michaels alone after Stock has moved. That works because the two halves of TuftStock share only the shop: Michaels orders become ordinary shop orders, which the suite's sync already counts as sales.

## 6. Could not determine, and things worth knowing

**Where TuftStock's own documents and its code disagree** (the tables follow the code):

- **"Never reorder by collection" is saved but never used.** Settings takes a list of collections (`src/components/SettingsForm.tsx:111`); the sync only looks at tags (`src/lib/sync/products.ts:35-57`).
- **The "daily sync time" box does nothing by itself.** It is saved (`src/components/SettingsForm.tsx:74`), but the time is set on the hosting service's timer (`docs/07-deploy.md:51`).
- **"Alerts" are listed as a non-goal** in `docs/01-product-spec.md`, yet the Michaels connector emails alerts (`src/lib/michaels/alerts.ts`). Nothing else in TuftStock sends an alert.
- **A shop with several stock locations**: the documents mention a picker in Settings; the choices are recorded (`src/lib/sync/location.ts:24`) but no picker was found.
- **The order list's "Export CSV"** exports the list of orders, not one order's lines; the per-order document is the PDF only.
- **Receiving**: the documents describe a separate "Update Shopify" button; the code does both in one press and keeps the separate address only for retries.

**Not checked, or could not be told from code:**

- **How many suppliers, items, open orders and ledger entries there are to move.** That is in TuftStock's database, not its code.
- **Whether the two apps give the same suggested amounts on the real catalogue.** The sums are the same code, but three inputs differ: sold-out days (the suite's own count only starts when it is switched on), cost (the same rule now, but the suite's average only knows deliveries checked in here), and case rounding. A side-by-side run on live data is the only real test.
- **What happens in the suite to an item deleted from the shop.** TuftStock marks it discontinued (`src/lib/sync/products.ts:123-134`); the suite sets aside items that are archived or not counted, but a deleted one was not traced.
- **Whether a new shop order reaches the suite faster than the hourly read** (for example pushed by the shop). Not traced.
- **Whether live QuickBooks answers the payment questions as written.** The connection's permission (`com.intuit.quickbooks.accounting`) covers them, and the suite now asks for bill payments and purchases (`quickbooks-payments.ts`), but it has only been run against made-up answers. (Its UPS connection is now used for tracking: UPS's Track API takes the same sign-in, once the Tracking product is added to the developer app.)
- **Which service the suite sends its own email with today.** (Orders to suppliers don't use it: they go from the business's own Gmail.)
- **The Michaels seller API** was not called; every Michaels row is from TuftStock's code and notes.
- **Whether the old app has a box for a supplier's days-to-pay.** The figure exists and is used; no box to change it was found.

**Things that were a surprise:**

- _(Since fixed.)_ **The suite's "To order" cards still suggest orders for suppliers marked "not used any more"**, while ticking the same items and pressing "draft" correctly leaves them out (`lib/plan.ts:323-328` against `:356`).
- _(Since fixed.)_ **Checking a delivery in has no double-press guard in the suite**, though drafting orders does (`stock/actions.ts:552-592`).
- _(Since shown.)_ **Two things were worked out in the suite but never shown**: the four late-money totals (`lib/balance/aging.ts`) and the "payment may not be in their statement yet" list (`lib/balance/balance.ts`). Both are on the Balances tab now. The database also has a bulk planning function no screen calls (`plan_stock_items`, `mig/20261203000100_stock.sql:267`); it overwrites every figure, so bulk edits were built on a new one (`set_stock_items_planning`) and it stays unused.
- **The suite's ledger has a slot for each way an entry arrives** (`source` is `manual`, `import` for a spreadsheet, or `books` for a payment read from QuickBooks), so an email reader, if one is ever built, has somewhere to land.
- **In a few places the suite is ahead**: suggestions rounded to whole cases, a cover figure per supplier, a plain-words explanation and a page per item, team roles, hourly rather than nightly reads, and settings that keep their history.
- **TuftStock is two tools in one.** The Michaels connector is about a quarter of the app's code, and shares nothing with the stock side but the shop connection. That is what makes "move Stock now, Michaels later" workable.
