# Help desk: the owner's help desk app vs. the suite, side by side

Written 25 Nov 2026 before the rebuild, and brought up to date after it, after the second pass, after the manual was built, and after the help centre and topic media. The old app was only read. Nothing here was checked against real mail or a real shop: see section 5.

**How to read the file references**

- **Old** paths are inside the owner's help desk repo (`public/js/…`, `src/…`).
- **New** paths are inside this repo: `desk/` means `src/app/w/[slug]/helpdesk/`, `lib/` means `src/lib/helpdesk/`, `mig/` means `supabase/migrations/`.
- **Matched** means the suite does it. **Partly** means some of it. **Missing** means it was looked for and not found.
- Size to close: **S** = a day or two, **M** = about a week, **L** = several weeks or a new integration.
- Shipping has its own file (`docs/shipping-side-by-side.md`) and is not counted here.

---

## 1. Summary

The old app has **126** help desk capabilities a person answering customers would notice.

- **Before the rebuild** (counted 25 Nov 2026): **18 matched**, **27 partly**, **81 missing**.
- **After the rebuild**: **76 matched**, **15 partly**, **35 missing**.
- **After the second pass** (files, Archive / Spam / Trash, bulk actions, undo send and the smaller items): **92 matched**, **11 partly**, **23 missing**.
- **After the manual** (topics written from conversations, the knowledge base, the picker in the reply box): **94 matched**, **13 partly**, **19 missing**.
- **After Instagram and Facebook messages** (direct messages as a channel, answered from the same reply box): **96 matched**, **14 partly**, **16 missing**.
- **After the help centre and topic media** (public help pages on the suite's own site, customers' photos and videos on topics, a line per case, joining and copying topics): **97 matched**, **13 partly**, **16 missing**.
- **After comments on posts** (comments on Instagram and Facebook posts as conversations, with a public reply or one private message, hide and delete): **98 matched**, **12 partly**, **16 missing**.
- **Recounted on 2026-11-27**: “Add to repair manual” is matched by the details rail's “Add this conversation”, and the automatic first reply had already made one Settings row partly matched: **99 matched**, **13 partly**, **14 missing**.

## 4. Repair manual and knowledge base: how the old one works, and what was built

**How it works in the old app.**

- _A topic_ is a title, one product (“machine”), a summary and a Markdown body that the AI writes under fixed headings (Symptoms, Likely causes, How to fix it, Parts & tools, Notes), plus draft/published, “use in AI drafts” and “edited by a person”. Each topic keeps its _cases_ (the ticket, one line on what happened, the outcome) and the photos and videos picked from those tickets, with captions. Symptoms are not a field: they are a heading in the body. The list is grouped by machine.
- _Which conversations_: closed, with a reply from the team, not read before, and either classified as a repair, tagged “Repairs”, or containing one of two dozen repair words written into the code (`REPAIR_WORDS`). The AI then decides whether each one really is a repair.
- _What is sent_: every message of up to four conversations at a time (the customer's name and address on each line), attachment names, up to three photos a conversation (eight a batch, 3.5 MB each), and the titles and summaries of up to 300 existing topics with their ids. A second call per touched topic rewrites the whole topic from the old text and the new cases.
- _New or existing_: the first call answers with an existing topic's id or a new topic's title; the second call rewrites it.
- _A person's edits_: saved in place and marked `edited_at`. The only protection afterwards is a sentence in the prompt asking the AI to keep their wording. There is no history for topics.
- _While answering_: the forty most recently updated published topics are added to every AI draft. There is no picker and nothing is shown beside a ticket.
- _Knowledge base_: customer-facing articles in HTML, published to the shop's blog (so the old app does have a public help centre, on the shop's own site), pulled back in from it, with pictures, link checks, near-duplicate merging, versions, and AI suggestions from conversations that a person accepts or dismisses.
- _Schedule and cost_: none for the manual. An admin presses “Read N new” or “Dig into archive” and the browser asks for batch after batch; “Add to repair manual” reads one ticket. Tokens are counted per feature; there is no cap.
- _Search_: in the browser, over title, machine and summary.

**What the suite has** (`src/lib/helpdesk/manual/`, pages under `(app)/helpdesk/manual/`, `supabase/migrations/20270109000100_helpdesk_manual.sql`).

- General, not about repairs: the name (“Manual” until a workspace names it), the first tab's name, a sentence on what belongs in it, the labels topics are sorted by and the headings they are written under are one setting (`helpdesk` / `manual`). So are the sources: folders, saved views or tags. No word list.
- A topic's body is the suite's small document; a bold one-line paragraph is a heading. Labels are free values under the workspace's facets.
- The worker reads one conversation per call: its words with quoted text left out and scrubbed (email addresses, phone-like numbers, order and tracking numbers, street addresses, the customer's name), attachment names, up to three of the customer's kept photos (JPEG, PNG, GIF or WebP under 3.5 MB, never a video; a workspace can switch photos off), and a shortlist of at most eight existing topics found by word matching, each under a reference of ours (T1…). The model never sees an id. Structured output, no tools.
- The answer is data: tidied, scrubbed again and planned in code. A new topic is a draft. Points are added under their headings and nothing written is changed. A topic a person has edited is never changed: the points wait as a suggested update. The database function that files a reading holds the same rule.
- Every change leaves a revision with who or what made it; any version can be brought back.
- By itself only where switched on (daily or weekly); otherwise on a manager's “Update now” (once per ten minutes, six a day) or “Add this conversation” (thirty a day). Ten conversations a run, a monthly cap the workspace sets (100 unless changed, 1000 at most), and the plan's AI allowance: it stops at zero and says so.
- While answering: the picker in the reply box, matching topics in the rail, and matching topics and articles handed to the AI draft.

**Photos, videos and cases on a topic** (`supabase/migrations/20270112000100_helpdesk_topic_media.sql`, `manual/topic-media.tsx`, `lib/helpdesk/manual/join.ts`).

- The old app: the first AI call names the attachments “worth showing” with a caption each (photos it was shown and videos it only knows by file name), and they go straight onto the topic; a person can recaption or remove one. Each conversation leaves a case (one sentence up to 500 characters, an outcome up to 200). Merging moves cases and media and deletes the first topic; the next AI update rewrites the combined text. “Copy for another machine” has the AI rewrite the topic for the target machine from other topics, articles and the shop's listing.
- The suite: when a reading files a conversation under a topic, the customer's kept pictures and videos from it become candidates (`manual_topic_media`: ids pointing at `message_attachments`, nothing copied). A person keeps, captions, orders and removes them. Claude may point at a picture it was shown, by a reference of ours (P1…), and propose a caption; that marks the candidate and prefills the box, and nothing is kept until a person presses. Videos are never sent and never arrive with a caption. The same limits: 500, 200 and 300 characters, scrubbed.
- They are served only by the help desk's private file route to signed-in members (every member of a workspace can already see every conversation, so no new rule was needed). When the attachment's row goes (a conversation deleted for good, a customer forgotten), the topic's entry goes with it.
- A case line and candidates for a topic a person has edited wait with the suggested update and are filed if it is accepted.
- Joining (owners and admins) is done by rule, not by a model: what the joined topic says that the other doesn't is added as points under the same headings, and both histories, the conversations, cases and media move across. A copy is the same words as a new draft under another label; nothing else comes with it.

**The help centre** (`src/app/help-centre/`, `lib/helpdesk/help-centre-server.ts`, `help-centre-gate.ts`, `lib/helpdesk/help-centre/`, `supabase/migrations/20270111000100_helpdesk_help_centre.sql`).

- The old app: an article is HTML, cleaned by an allowlist of tags, with pictures pulled out into its own store. “Publish” writes one blog article per published article to the shop's Knowledge Base blog through the shop's API (title, body, a summary, tags by topic, SEO title and description), a few at a time; “pull” brings the shop's versions back and overwrites the desk's unless it has unpublished changes; link checks compare every link with what is really on the shop; an AI finds near-duplicates and merges them; every change saves an earlier version; an AI reads closed conversations and files suggestions a person accepts into an article.
- The suite: an article has a public state (for the team; a help article in draft; published), an address of its own that is one of a kind in the workspace (old addresses redirect), a category, a line for lists and a last-updated date. Only owners and admins make one public, publish or unpublish, and only they change one that is published. Published articles are read at `/help-centre/<workspace>` and `/help-centre/<workspace>/<article>` on the suite's own site: the workspace's name and logo, categories in its order, word search (no model, thirty a minute a visitor), the article drawn from the small document as text (https links only, `rel="nofollow noopener"`), its own pictures from a public bucket, “Was this helpful?” (counted once a visitor an article a day, no cookie), a contact line, a sitemap, and `noindex` if the workspace asks.
- Nothing else is reachable: the pages read through three database functions that return published articles of one named workspace whose help centre is on. A page that isn't there is a real 404, checked before anything is drawn.
- A topic is never public. “Make a help article from this topic” copies its words into a new draft article; “Draft a help article with Claude” has Claude rewrite the topic (never the conversations) for customers, as a draft, counted against the AI allowance. Customers' photos are never copied.
- Not built: publishing to the shop's blog. It needs the shop permission to write blog articles (`write_content`; the live shop connection reads only), the owner's go-ahead for that, the once-only queue the other writes to a shop go through with a manager's press each time, the body written as HTML by us from the small document, and the pictures given by full address from our public bucket. Also not built: pulling pages in (still against “nothing a person types is fetched”), link checking, near-duplicate merging, article versions, and suggestions read straight from conversations.

## 5. Not checked against the real thing

- No real email was sent or received: sending goes through the same mail service as before, with Cc, Bcc, an HTML part and attachments added to the request, and that request is covered by tests, not by a delivery.
- No real shop was read: the customer and orders in the rail were looked at with made-up rows shaped like what the sync stores.
- Claude's summary and the draft hint were not run against the API here; the prompts and the tidying are tested.
- The migrations run in the test database here; applying them elsewhere is done outside this repo.
- No real email with attachments was received: the mail service's attachment fields (name, content, type, content id, length) are read as its documentation describes them, and tested with made-up payloads. How each mail app marks a set-in picture in its plain text (“[cid:…]”, “[image: name]”) is from the apps we know of; one that leaves no mark gets its picture under the message instead.
- Storage itself is not in the test database: the bucket's row and its policy are tested, uploading and downloading are not.
- Undo send needs the worker running; the wait, taking back, a refusal and a send that never finished are tested in the database, not against the mail service.
- The owner's “only the signature showed” email wasn't available. The cause found in the code (the mail service's own guess at “just the reply” was preferred over the whole text) is fixed and tested with made-up emails.
- The manual's reader was never run against the API: the prompt, the tidying, the scrubbing, the planning and the worker's path are tested with a made-up reader, including a conversation that tries to give orders. How good the topics are, how well Claude files a conversation under an existing topic, and what a real update costs in tokens are not known.
- Photos are read from storage and sent as pictures in tests with made-up bytes only; storage isn't in the test database.
- Scrubbing was tested with invented names, numbers and addresses. Order-number and address shapes it hasn't seen (and names other than the ticket's own customer) can get through, which is why new topics are drafts for a person to read.
- No real conversations were used: which folder, view or tag holds the owner's repair conversations in the suite is for the owner to choose.
- The help centre was looked at only against a made-up workspace on a fake server: the home page, an article, search, a vote, the 404s and the old-address redirect, at desktop and phone width. It has not been opened on the real site, behind the real proxy (the visitor's address for rate limits comes from `x-forwarded-for`), or by a search engine.
- Uploading an article's picture was not run end to end: storage isn't in the test database or the fake server. The bucket's row, the checks on a registered picture and its removal queue are tested.
- “Draft a help article with Claude” and Claude's picture picks and case lines were never run against the API: the prompts, tidying and scrubbing are tested with made-up answers.
- A video on a topic plays through a one-minute storage link; that was not tried with a real file.
